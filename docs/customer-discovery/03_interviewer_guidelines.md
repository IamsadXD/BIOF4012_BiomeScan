# BiomeScan interviewer guidelines

Working guide for BIOF4012 student co-investigators. Prepared 3 November 2026. Use with the interview questions and investigator-confirmed information and consent materials. The research tests an unmet problem; it does not assume the proposed technology is useful.

## Recruit the appropriate participant

Choose one topic: invasive-plant management, farm weed inspection, or standing-water and mosquito-prevention inspection. Recruit someone with recent direct experience doing, supervising or commissioning that work. Ask for role names rather than guessing individual employees' addresses. Under the supplied information sheet, participants must be Hong Kong residents aged 18 or above. Government or employer permission does not replace individual consent, and individual consent does not authorize confidential workplace disclosure.

Use the problem-first email templates. State the course's customer-discovery and business-hypothesis purpose accurately. Keep the product name, pitch deck, demo, sensors, proposed outputs and prices out of recruitment. If directly asked about the idea, answer honestly and offer to discuss it at the end. Do not describe this as unrelated academic research to obscure its business purpose.

## Consent and preparation

Before participant use, resolve the review fields in the consent draft with the course investigators. The supplied documents conflict on retention, the option to be identified, bundled recording consent and the HREC telephone number. The source information sheet promises anonymized reporting and limits eligibility. The adaptation separates participation and audio choices, omits photography/video and leaves policy fields open for confirmation; it does not claim those changes are already approved.

Send the confirmed participant information before the interview. Complete the permitted written or oral consent procedure. Ask participation and audio questions separately. Do not start recording before permission. Use a notes-only route only if permitted. Ask the participant to avoid identifiable client/resident details, confidential incidents and sensitive site or contract information. They may skip any question.

Prepare an interview code, the correct topic wording, eight core questions and at most four optional questions. Arrange interviewer and note-taker roles where a second authorized student is available. Store consent identities separately from anonymized analysis using the confirmed storage arrangement.

## Run a 15 to 20 minute conversation

Allow around two minutes for consent and context, 12–15 minutes for the problem and purchasing questions, and up to one or two minutes for an optional concept discussion. Ask about one recent real event. Listen about 80 percent of the time, as the sample recommends. Let silence work. Useful neutral probes include “What happened next?”, “Could you give an example?” and “What, if anything, was difficult?”

Accept “the current process works well.” Ask about attempts already made, recent decisions and actual resource allocations before hypothetical preferences. Keep inspection, identification, clearance/treatment, reporting and repeat-visit time separate. Accept broad cost ranges or refusals. Do not anchor answers with the pitch's prices, offer desired answers, turn a priority score into buying intent, or collect only favorable comments.

Establish the user, action owner, approver and payer. A technical adviser may have no budget. Ask how a comparable service was last specified, approved and paid for. For government, ask about the applicable purchasing route and evidence requirements without seeking confidential tender information or commitments. Public-sector advice or a staff interview is not departmental endorsement.

## Select a small number of sector probes

**Construction and property:** Which locations are difficult to check? In the last case, were consequential water sources exposed or concealed? Who closes a finding, and is inspection already included in a maintenance or pest-control contract? Distinguish locating problems from insufficient clearance capacity or delayed coordination.

**Pest-control operators:** Which step used most staff time? What evidence did the client need before accepting work? Are extra inspections separately chargeable? Establish whether value accrues to the operator, client or both.

**Ecology and conservation:** Which invasive plants actually required attention on the last project? How are uncertain identifications checked? How was regrowth assessed? What existing maps or monitoring records were sufficient? Ask about Mikania only after the participant names their actual species and tasks.

**Farm owners and managers:** Which weeds mattered last cycle? Was finding them difficult, or did repeated removal take the time? Establish production area, crop cycle, staffing and who approves outside field services. Do not assume Mikania is present or that the farm needs or can afford aerial surveys. Crop-pest diagnosis and treatment remain outside the proposed service scope.

**Government:** Which team owns the programme, specifies specialist work and assesses the evidence? Does an existing contract cover the task? How is comparable work normally commissioned? Separate the department's role from its incumbent contractor and avoid treating one end budget as two customer markets.

## Explain the concept only at the end

Complete the problem questions and ask for any final additions first. If the confirmed study scope permits it, ask whether the participant wants the final minute to hear the idea. Use only the selected topic's description from the interview document. State that the relevant service remains unvalidated. Do not present simulation scores as Hong Kong field performance, a habitat map as mosquito detection, or a new species as covered by the Mikania model.

Ask where the idea would fit poorly and what evidence would be required to consider it. Keep that reaction separate from the unprompted problem evidence. A compliment, site visit or introduction is not evidence of a purchase. Do not negotiate a price, promise a pilot or show a long demo during the final minute. Ask separately about a permitted follow-up contact.

## Debrief and record evidence

In the restricted analysis record, capture the interview code, date, segment, role, recent example, current method, burden, alternatives tried, priorities, payer, purchasing route, negative evidence and permitted next step. Distinguish statements from interpretation and evidence before or after the concept. Record unknowns.

Generalize identifying organizations, roles and sites before reporting, as required by the supplied anonymity wording. Track contacts, replies and completed interviews separately. The schedule proposes five distinct businesses/farm operators contacted each week; it cannot guarantee five interviews. Farm trading names are not verified legal-company registrations. Keep government/NGO contacts separate if the rubric requires companies.

Follow the confirmed withdrawal and recording-erasure procedure. Keep completed forms, identities, correspondence and interview records outside Git. Course consent does not authorize commercial reuse or marketing; later pilots, site access and commercial work require separate arrangements.
