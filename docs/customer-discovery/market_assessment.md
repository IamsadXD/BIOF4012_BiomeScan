# BiomeScan market validation and contacts

Research prepared 2 November 2026 and organized into this repository on 3 November 2026 for the BIOF4012 student team. Hong Kong is the first market. This is a prospecting and interview plan; no organization has been contacted and no buying interest has been established.

## What demand is actually supported

There is demonstrable demand for invasive-plant management and mosquito-prevention work. Demand for an additional specialist survey, hyperspectral sensing, a software subscription or a reference library remains unproven. Start by finding a costly decision or inspection failure inside an existing workflow, then establish who could pay to improve it.

The strongest near-term commercial hypothesis is that some construction sites have costly gaps in standing-water inspection and follow-up. Contractors and their pest-control providers are accessible interview targets. This is a prioritization judgment, not evidence that they want aerial surveys. The Mikania hypothesis has clearer direct biological relevance but potentially fewer independent buyers and longer routes to purchase. Interview the two segments separately.

### Evidence that work already exists

1. **Mikania management is recurring work.** AFCD describes manual/mechanical clearance as usual practice and notes that regrowth can require repeat clearing. Its biodiversity page describes regular monitoring and removal. This supports questions about finding patches, deciding priorities and checking regrowth. It does not establish that identification is currently unreliable or that managers lack maps. Sources: [AFCD Mikania guidance](https://www.afcd.gov.hk/english/conservation/con_flo/About_Mikania/about_mikania.html), [AFCD invasive alien species](https://www.afcd.gov.hk/english/conservation/hkbiodiversity/Invasive_Alien_Species/ias.html).
2. **There is contracted work involving invasive vegetation.** The 2024 Long Valley landscape-maintenance tender explicitly includes physical removal of Mikania and other invasive plants, identified by the government representative, in its service specifications. This establishes a contracted task, not a separate budget for detection. The tender is historical and is not an advertised opportunity to bid now. [Tender AFCD/NP/02/24, service specifications, printed page 142](https://www.afcd.gov.hk/english/tender/tender_con/files/AFCDNP0224.pdf).
3. **Standing-water inspection is routine operational work.** FEHD advises construction-site management to assign responsible staff and check for water accumulation at least weekly. These are published recommendations; do not recast them as a legal interpretation. [FEHD mosquito guidance](https://www.fehd.gov.hk/english/pestcontrol/risk-pest-mosquito.html).
4. **The problem has recent operational and enforcement attention.** A government release dated 22 September 2026 reports more than 65,000 FEHD inspections, 14 prosecutions and 63 stagnant-water notices in August 2026. Those totals cover FEHD activity generally, not construction sites alone. This is evidence of activity, not a forecast of a survey market. [Government release](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202609/22/P2026092200636.htm).
5. **Customers already buy competing services.** Rentokil describes premises inspection and continuing mosquito-management programmes. Existing providers may already solve the inspection problem economically. Interview them as possible customers, channels and competitors. [Rentokil mosquito services](https://www.rentokil.com/hk/en/services/pest-control-services/mosquitoes).

### What each customer might actually need

| Segment | Job to investigate | Possible payer to identify | Evidence that would weaken the hypothesis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Construction main contractor | Finish post-rain checks, locate actionable water and confirm clearance | Project manager or environmental/site-services budget owner | Walking checks are quick; problem is blocked drainage or delayed action; most consequential water is concealed |
| Pest-control operator | Scope jobs, reduce difficult searches and repeat visits, provide useful evidence to clients | Operations manager or client through an existing contract | Survey adds cost while treatment is fixed-price; inspection already works; client will not pay for extra reporting |
| Property manager | Complete roof, podium and grounds inspections; resolve repeated findings | Estate owner, owners' corporation or facilities budget | Most hotspots are small concealed containers; flight/access effort exceeds inspection effort; manager cannot approve extra spend |
| Ecology consultancy | Deliver a vegetation baseline or repeat monitoring to a client's specification | Consultancy project director within a client-funded scope | Existing botanist/GIS work is sufficient; client does not accept the output; no recoverable subcontract budget |
| AFCD or another land manager | Select clearance locations and check regrowth or restoration outcomes | Programme owner through the applicable purchasing route | Clearance capacity is the bottleneck; sufficient records already exist; improved location information changes no decision |
| Conservation organization | Manage a particular site with limited staff and show results | Site programme, project funder or sponsor | Strong ecological interest but no budget; volunteer work is adequate; repeat surveys would never be commissioned |

Keep identification, locating, treatment and evidence/reporting costs separate. A large clearance or pest-control bill does not mean a large mapping budget. If the problem is action after discovery, better detection may have little value.

### What the README changes about the pitch

The current README describes a Mikania research demonstrator with simulated Hong Kong survey scenes and treatment outcomes. Aedes habitat surveying and further species remain planned workstreams. The newer pitch is a platform concept, not evidence of field performance or customer demand. Its pricing, coverage, annual revenue and accuracy figures are assumptions to test.

Do not repeat the deck's assertions that nothing measures habitat, that there is no efficacy loop, or that a whole public estate is the addressable paid market. Ask what already exists. Do not assume a sensor observes concealed containers, identifies mosquitoes or replaces a field check. Animals, other insects, broad farm platforms and data licensing need their own discovery; they are lower priorities for the initial six-week discovery exercise.

## What counts as evidence

**Weak:** compliments, interest in drones, an expert interview, introductions, or permission to visit a site. **Problem evidence:** a specific recent event, recurring frequency and measurable consequences. **Buying-process evidence:** a known user, budget owner, existing spending and procurement route. **Later commercial evidence:** a separately agreed evaluation scope and eventual paid repeat work. A course research interview and consent do not authorize a paid pilot, data licensing or reuse of identifiable research records for sales.

Accept negative results. In particular, establish whether relevant water is visible and difficult to inspect, whether managers can act sooner, and whether useful species-level information changes clearance priorities. These are the assumptions that decide whether BiomeScan has a viable initial service.
