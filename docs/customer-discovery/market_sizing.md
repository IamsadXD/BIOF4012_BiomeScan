# BiomeScan market sizing (TAM / SAM / SOM)

Prepared 12 November 2026. These are bottom-up estimates: official counts multiplied by labelled assumptions. No customer, willingness to pay or price has been validated. Every price below is an assumption to test in interviews, not evidence.

## Summary

On current assumptions, the Hong Kong market for the services the current design could deliver (SAM) is **HK$8.5-18.0M a year**. It is dominated by standing-water surveys at large and medium construction sites. A broader Hong Kong plus Guangdong total (TAM), counting every eligible site and hectare as if all of them bought, is **HK$108-245M a year (about US$14-31M at HK$7.8)**. A plausible year-3 share (SOM) is **HK$0.5-1.2M a year**, about 6-7% of SAM. The Mikania segment is small because the only official Hong Kong area figure is about 100 ha, from 2002. The repository's 3,000 ha programme has no local support.

## Method and pricing assumptions

Each segment is a count from an official source multiplied by frequency and price. All three prices are **assumptions**:

- Vegetation survey: HK$500-1,200 per hectare. This is the repository's modelling figure; no public Hong Kong price was found.
- Standing-water site survey: HK$3,000-6,000 per site visit. No public Hong Kong drone day rate was found.
- Consultancy vegetation-mapping subcontract: HK$30,000-80,000 per project.

Contract values are annualised by dividing the award by its term. Ranges are rounded to the nearest HK$0.1M.

## Sensitivity

The construction visit count moves SAM more than any other single assumption. Each extra wet-season visit per site changes SAM by HK$0.8-1.6M. Weekly checks in place of monthly ones, as FEHD guidance recommends, would multiply that segment roughly fourfold. Even at monthly frequency, the HK$3,000-6,000 price range alone swings SAM by HK$5.5M. The other assumptions matter much less:

- Each 5-point change in the consultancy share moves SAM by HK$0.2-0.6M.
- Each 1x change in the Mikania search multiple moves SAM by HK$0.1-0.24M.

Visit frequency and price per visit should therefore be the first things tested in interviews.

## Constraints and substitutes

- **FEHD already flies its own drones.** FEHD reports using drones with 3D mapping to find concealed stagnant water and breeding sites (LegCo paper, April 2026). Government demand for standing-water mapping may be met in-house.
- **Flying is a commodity.** 164 organisations hold CAD Advanced Operations Permission. They include likely customers such as Gammon, Hip Hing, Build King, Urban Property Management and Savills Project Consultancy. Any differentiation has to come from analysis, verified reference data and evidence that supports decisions, not from flight time.
- **Penalties cap what contractors will pay.** A mosquito-breeding conviction carries a maximum fine of HK$25,000 plus HK$450 a day. FEHD brought 95 construction-site prosecutions in January-August 2025. A survey costing more than the expected fine needs another justification.
- **There is no current Mikania area data.** About 100 ha was affected in 2002, and all departments together cleared 4.3 ha in 2012. In 2013 the government stated that area statistics "are not available" and that a cost breakdown was not feasible. The Mikania SAM line rests on a 24-year-old figure and an assumed multiple.
- **The technology is not proven in the field.** The Mikania demonstrator runs on simulated scenes. Foliage-only accuracy is 68.7% for VNIR hyperspectral against 53.1% for RGB. Leave-one-site-out accuracy is 80.4%, with a 95% CI of 50.8-98.0%. The Aedes workstream is planned, not built, and would detect visible standing water only.
- **Demand drivers are real but are not survey budgets:**
  - FEHD spent HK$811M on pest and rodent control in 2025-26.
  - Hong Kong recorded 161 dengue cases in 2024 and 82 chikungunya cases in 2025 (11 local).
  - FEHD lowered its gravidtrap action threshold to 10% in August 2025.
  - Shenzhen spent CNY148M treating 347,000 mu of Mikania in 2021-25.
  - Guangdong allocated CNY319M for forestry pest control, Mikania included, in 2024-25.

## What would change the numbers

These interview findings would replace assumptions with evidence:

1. **Construction:** how often sites check for standing water now, who does it, what it costs, and whether the consequential water is visible from the air. These set frequency and price, the dominant SAM terms.
2. **Pest-control operators:** whether a survey would be bought by them or by their client, or would sit unpaid inside a fixed-price contract.
3. **Ecology consultancies:** the share of recent projects that commissioned vegetation mapping, and the budget for it.
4. **AFCD and NGO site managers:** whether any current contract funds survey or monitoring, and whether better maps would change clearance priorities.
5. **Mikania extent:** any current area records held by AFCD, LCSD or Lands Department. Without them, the Mikania line should stay an explicit assumption.

A single paid repeat engagement would be stronger evidence than any of these estimates.
